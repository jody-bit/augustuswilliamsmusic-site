How Not To Suck

A practical electronic music workshop on discipline, live hardware, better decisions, and staying teachable long enough to get good.

Augustus Williams · Tec-Troit Education Series · Detroit

How Not To Suck is a workshop built from more than two decades of real-world electronic music experience: clubs, studios, labels, hardware, mistakes, recovery, and the long process of becoming useful to your own ideas.

It is not about shortcuts. It is not about fame. It is not a gear-shopping fantasy. It is about the habits that actually matter when the novelty wears off: finishing music, preparing for rooms, listening better, asking better questions, and learning how to keep moving.

Augustus Williams approaches the workshop as a Detroit techno artist, DJ, producer, live-hardware performer, A&R, mentor, and Metroplex recording artist. The goal is simple: pass forward the practical knowledge that usually only shows up in side conversations, studio visits, and late-night problem-solving.

What You Learn

  • How to finish more music than you abandon.
  • How to separate useful gear from expensive distraction.
  • How to prepare for DJ, hybrid, and live-hardware sets.
  • How to listen for pressure, movement, and room energy.
  • How to avoid common traps that stall new producers.
  • How to respect Detroit techno lineage without getting frozen by it.
  • How to stay curious, disciplined, and teachable.

Workshop Themes

Each session can adapt to the room, but the foundation stays the same: practical tools, honest context, and less mythology.

Creative Discipline

Finishing tracks, building habits, escaping endless loops, and learning how to work when inspiration is unreliable.

Gear Reality

What matters, what does not, what to buy later, and why tools only become useful after you learn them.

Live Performance

DJ sets, hybrid sets, live hardware, preparation, risk, recovery, and learning how to read the room.

Studio Workflow

Turning fragments into finished work, making decisions faster, and building repeatable creative systems.

Releasing Music

Labels, self-release, promotion, patience, and thinking beyond the first upload or one-off moment.

Passing It Forward

Detroit techno grows when knowledge moves from one generation to the next instead of being locked away.

Format

Designed for festivals, schools, community programs, artist-development events, and music-technology spaces.

Length

60 to 90 minutes, with room for Q&A and audience participation.

Mode

Talk, gear demo, live beat build, practical examples, and open questions.

Audience

Beginner and intermediate producers, DJs, hardware-curious artists, young musicians, and electronic music fans who want to understand the work behind the sound.

Goal

Leave people with fewer myths, better questions, and a clearer path toward making and finishing their own work.
