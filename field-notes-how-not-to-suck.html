How Not To Suck

Lessons from 20+ years of making electronic music, learning the hard way, and passing the useful parts forward.

Augustus Williams · Tec-Troit Education Series · June 2026

How Not To Suck started as a workshop title, but the idea is bigger than one event.

It is a practical conversation about electronic music: how to keep learning, how to make better choices, how to avoid wasting years on the wrong things, and how to build a creative practice that can survive past the first rush of inspiration.

Augustus Williams has spent more than two decades inside Detroit electronic music as a fan, DJ, producer, live-hardware performer, A&R, mentor, and Metroplex recording artist. This workshop turns that experience into something direct, useful, and honest.

What This Is

This is not a shortcut. It is not a fame manual. It is not a gear-shopping fantasy.

It is about the habits that actually matter: finishing music, listening better, preparing for rooms, respecting the culture, asking better questions, and staying curious long enough to improve.

Core Lessons

  • Finish more music than you abandon.
  • Do not confuse buying gear with building skill.
  • Learn your tools before chasing new ones.
  • Prepare for the room, not the fantasy version of the room.
  • Respect the lineage, but do not become trapped by nostalgia.
  • Ask questions. Take notes. Stay teachable.
  • Do not wait until you feel ready to start doing the work.

Why It Matters

Detroit techno survives because knowledge moves. Somebody shows somebody else how to patch a machine, how to count a phrase, how to read a room, how to listen for pressure, how to keep going.

That exchange does not always happen on a stage. Often it happens in studios, living rooms, record shops, side conversations, late nights, and small moments that do not look historic while they are happening.

The workshop is part of that tradition. It is one artist handing back what was handed to him.
